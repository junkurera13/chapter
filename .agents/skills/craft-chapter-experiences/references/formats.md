# Experience formats

## Separate scale from social composition

Do not model `group` beside `small`, `mini`, and `proper` as though all four
describe the same property.

Scale describes commitment:

- `small`
- `mini`
- `proper`

Social composition describes who lives it:

- `self`
- `known-person`
- `new-person`
- `small-group`

A visible format is a curated combination of scale, company, structure, effort,
geography, energy, and time character.

## Launch scale contracts

### Small activity

Promise: "I could actually do this soon."

- Duration: roughly 30–90 minutes.
- Structure: one sharp action or compact ritual.
- Geography: normally neighbourhood or nearby city.
- Effort: spontaneous; avoid complicated booking.
- Cost: low enough that accepting does not require deliberation.
- Failure: a generic errand, ordinary meal, or trivialised version of a larger
  experience.

### Mini adventure

Promise: "This changes the shape of an afternoon."

- Duration: roughly 2–4 hours.
- Structure: one destination or activity, optionally supported by one natural
  beat.
- Geography: within the city or a modest journey.
- Effort: light preparation or a simple booking is acceptable.
- Failure: a list of unrelated stops or a small activity padded with travel.

### Proper adventure

Promise: "This deserves a day."

- Duration: a substantial half-day or full day.
- Structure: a coherent journey or short sequence with a clear arc.
- Geography: may reach beyond the city when the journey is part of the value.
- Effort: deliberate planning is acceptable only when every dependency is
  verified.
- Failure: a conventional itinerary, exhausting checklist, or distant venue
  without a reason the travel matters.

## Social composition contracts

### Self

Make solitude purposeful rather than treating it as the absence of company.
Avoid experiences that become unsafe, conspicuous, or logistically fragile
alone.

### Known person

Use only the initiator's private graph for an ordinary invitation, or the
privacy-safe Together cut when both worlds inform the plan. Give the pair
something to do together; do not force them into a conversation prompt.

### New person

Read [social-connection.md](social-connection.md). Spend the primary novelty
budget on the person. Keep the rest familiar and low-pressure; do not add a
supporting context to a first meeting.

### Small group

Use a shared task, discovery, or playful constraint that benefits from several
people. Verify capacity, coordination, cost, and whether the experience still
works if attendance changes.

## Pack composition

Use one card at each launch scale until evidence supports a wider deck:

1. one small activity;
2. one mini adventure;
3. one proper adventure.

Choose an internal basis independently:

- `world`: current local reality and Chapter's editorial taste, with no graph
  anchors;
- `graph`: one strong private thread, with real graph anchors; or
- `social`: one real person and only the permitted shared anchors.

Use exactly two world-led cards. The third card is graph-led when no real
social candidate exists, and social-led when one does. Do not show this
taxonomy as labels in the interface.

Choose social composition independently. When no safe and eligible social
context exists, substitute another composition instead of producing an
unusable card.

Keep face-down cards visually equivalent if the interaction is meant to conceal
both format and content. Reveal the format with the experience.

## Future format recipes

Do not treat these as launch commitments:

- after dark;
- seasonal;
- bring someone;
- someone new;
- out of town;
- return to something;
- small-group side quest.

Add a format only when it changes the design and research contract, not merely
the card colour or title.
