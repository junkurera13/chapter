# Designing experiences for human connection

## Purpose

Use experiences to create the conditions for connection. Do not predict the
relationship that will result.

Known friends may deepen their relationship. Strangers may become friends.
Love may emerge. None of those outcomes may be promised, scored, or inferred
from private memories.

## Privacy boundaries

For a connected pair, plan only from the shareable cut produced by
`planningGraphFrom` in `lib/togetherGeneration.ts` when both graphs inform the
experience. That cut contains places, activities, and interests only.

For strangers, reduce further to the strict intersection of the two shareable
cuts. Every pre-consent sentence must already be true in both worlds.

Never reveal:

- raw memories or evidence;
- people, feelings, conditions, or patterns;
- a one-sided node or inference;
- the other person's name, face, answer, or supposed compatibility before
  mutual consent.

Composition of a Together chapter belongs to the initiator alone.

After mutual consent, the opposite rule applies to an actionable experience:
show the actual person. A social card must carry a server-confirmed person and
render their real first name. Never write `someone new`, `a stranger`,
`someone you know`, `a friend`, `bring someone`, or another anonymous
substitute where a person should be.

If there is no specific eligible person, make the card solo. Do not create a
social-shaped placeholder and hope matching happens later.

## First-meeting experience contract

Keep the person as the single stretch. Make everything else maximally familiar
and easy to leave.

Require:

- a specific mutually-consented person attached before experience generation;
- a public, populated setting;
- a bounded duration;
- an activity that lets people stand or work side by side;
- natural conversation openings without interview-like pressure;
- a clear arrival point and simple logistics;
- an easy, dignified exit;
- a worthwhile experience even if no connection forms.

Reject:

- isolated routes, private homes, or dependency on one person's transport;
- activities requiring physical trust or intimate contact;
- alcohol as the mechanism of connection;
- high cost, high commitment, or irreversible booking;
- an audience or performance that prevents interaction;
- copy implying destiny, matching scores, attraction, or compatibility.

## Friends, strangers, and groups

Treat experiences with existing friends as immediately usable.

Treat stranger experiences as opportunistic. Offer them only when a valid
same-city candidate and mutual-consent pathway exist. When the pool cannot
support one, substitute another social composition; never show a dead card.

Treat group formation as a density-dependent runtime capability. Keep it in
the design system now, but do not assume enough eligible people exist.

## Romance

Keep ordinary introductions activity-framed and relationship-undefined.
Romance is a possible outcome, never the reason Chapter claims two people
should meet.

Do not design an explicitly dating-oriented flow without product-level age,
intent, orientation, blocking, reporting, moderation, and consent decisions.
